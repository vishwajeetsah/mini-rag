The Taj Mahal is an ivory-white marble mausoleum on the southern bank of the Yamuna river in Agra, India. It was commissioned in 1632 by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan to house the tomb of his favourite wife, Mumtaz Mahal. Construction of the main mausoleum was completed around 1648, with the surrounding complex finished about five years later. The monument combines Islamic, Persian, Ottoman Turkish and Indian architectural styles and is widely regarded as the greatest example of Mughal architecture. In 1983, the Taj Mahal was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Over 20,000 artisans and labourers are believed to have worked on its construction under the guidance of chief architect Ustad Ahmad Lahauri.
